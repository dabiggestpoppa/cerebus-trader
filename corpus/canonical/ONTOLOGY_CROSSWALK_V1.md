## Generational interpretation

### Generation A — Constraint Resolution
Primary question: what unresolved constraint exists and how can it resolve?
Keep as explanatory foundation.

### Generation B — Atomic / ST / P90
Primary question: what scale/tier and activation event indicate resolution?
Keep operator semantics; ST manual, P90 historical automation-capable lineage.

### Generation C — V4 / recursive / checkpoints
Primary question: how does the path evolve through time, failure, repair and repeated resolution?
Absorbed into path/state-age/range-left doctrine.

### Generation D — Workbook / weekly / rekey
Primary question: what is the order, timing and depth of deliveries across larger windows?
Keep lineage; exact historical claims require identity-safe evidence.

### Generation E — Markov / ASE / semi-Markov
Primary question: which states/transitions survive falsification and does dwell time matter?
Old Markov prior simulation is not empirical fitted proof; semi-Markov/state-age concepts survive.

### Generation F — Scientific cross-layer
Primary question: which layer owns destination, path and execution, and what survives OOS?
Foundation of current evidence hierarchy.

### Generation G — Modern CEREBUS
Primary question:
Given market-native scale, clock, state, path consumed, event family and remaining distribution, what outcomes remain available and what evidence supports them?

## FORGE translation rule
When answering historical questions, preserve original term and generation.
When answering current/operator questions, translate to canonical vocabulary and disclose historical aliases when useful.
Never move statistics across aliases without evidence that event definitions and populations are equivalent.
