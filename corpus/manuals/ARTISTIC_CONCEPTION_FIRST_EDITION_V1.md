# Volume I — Foundations

## Chapter 1: The Foundational Claims

> **Status:** Canonical reconstruction from the canvas. The claims in this chapter are preserved as the author’s framework and are not independently verified market facts.

## 1.1 Purpose of the foundation layer

The canvas begins by treating trading as a problem of structure, state, distance, and time rather than randomness alone. Its foundational branch is not yet an execution strategy. It supplies the assumptions from which the later ontology, session models, and playbooks are derived.

The foundation can be read as a dependency chain:

```text
Trading
  → Laws of Trading
  → Truths of Trading
  → Definitions
  → Perfect-Data / Fibonacci evidence branch
  → T.E.L.I.T. execution ontology
```

## 1.2 Laws of trading

The canvas states two primary laws:

1. **There is no randomness.**
2. **Certainty comes before probability.**

These are axioms of the framework, not conclusions proven inside the canvas. Their operational implication is that probability should be applied only after a market state, range, or sequence has been structurally identified.

### Interpretation boundary

The first law should not be rewritten as “every market movement is predictable.” The source is asserting that observed movement belongs to an underlying dynamical process. The second law appears to distinguish **state identification** from **outcome likelihood**: first determine what state the system occupies; then estimate the probability of its resolution.

## 1.3 Truth of trading: state carries information

The central definition reads:

> The market is a dynamical model with positional information encoded by distance to bifurcation.

The canvas immediately translates this into simpler language: the system’s position or state contains information about how close it is to changing modes.

This produces four foundational objects:

- **State:** the market’s present structural condition.
- **Position:** where price sits relative to defined limits or transitions.
- **Distance:** how far the present state is from a boundary or bifurcation.
- **Bifurcation:** the point at which the system changes behavior or resolution path.

## 1.4 Recursive price process

Price is defined as a recursive process in which each new observation references prior states and generates self-similar motion across timeframes.

This claim is used later to justify:

- nested patterns;
- higher-timeframe lattices containing lower-timeframe failures;
- repeated Fibonacci structures;
- session, daily, weekly, monthly, and quarterly resets;
- the idea that an incomplete sequence may resolve inside a stronger structure.

## 1.5 Perfect-data hypothesis

A separate foundation node proposes a “Perfect 1.0× Multiplier Match” and gives the identities:

```text
HIGH = LOW + (RANGE × 1.0)
LOW  = HIGH − (RANGE × 1.0)
```

These equations are algebraic identities when `RANGE = HIGH − LOW`. Therefore, by themselves, they do not establish predictive power. The meaningful research question is whether a previously defined range projects a future high or low at a 1.0× extension. The canvas’s broader statistical branch appears intended to supply that predictive context, but the exact experiment definition must remain attached to each claim.

### Canonical status

- The equations are preserved exactly.
- The phrase “nearly 100% of price movements” remains an **unverified canvas claim**.
- The distinction between identity and forecast must be preserved in any AI response.

## 1.6 Fibonacci and measured-move evidence branch

The foundation cluster contains a dense research node with claims about Fibonacci highs, ILM zones, measured moves, re-key paths, loops, temporal symmetry, and higher-timeframe aggregation. These records support later concepts but should not be taught as one undifferentiated law.

Representative source claims include:

- weekly highs forming at the 72% level at a stated rate;
- ILM touches leading to measured-move completions at a stated rate;
- target failures increasing the probability of lower-timeframe micro-legs;
- Fibonacci retest loops having different frequencies and post-loop sequences;
- WEZ presence increasing re-key success;
- alignment across ILM variants conditioning higher-timeframe behavior.

Each numerical claim remains `stated_in_canvas_unverified` until its dataset, sample, timeframe, and calculation can be reproduced.

## 1.7 Laws of the market

A later node expands the foundation into five market laws:

1. The cause of market movements is temporal.
2. Traded patterns persist individually; a failed completion exists within the extremes of a stronger lattice.
3. Market structure is set within range-limit extremes that reset at session, daily, weekly, monthly, and quarterly intervals.
4. The market has a collective memory that seeks to contain all of its states.
5. Once a sequence is initiated, there is no secondary event; indications are described as perfect and resolving immediately.

### Normalized interpretation

The first four laws form a coherent conceptual model: time creates state changes; state changes are expressed inside nested ranges; failed lower-level structures may be absorbed by stronger structures; and prior states constrain later resolution.

The fifth law is materially ambiguous. “No secondary event,” “perfect indications,” and “resolve immediately” require definitions and testing criteria before they can become executable logic. It is therefore retained as a **high-priority reconstruction gap**, not a canonical trading instruction.

## 1.8 Foundation-to-execution handoff

The foundation does not directly specify entries or exits. Its role is to define the objects that the execution ontology will use:

```text
Temporal cause
  → state and distance
  → range limits and bifurcation
  → sequence initiation or failure
  → delivery and closure
```

## 2.3 Entry

### Source-grounded role

Entry is the first operational state produced by T.E.L.I.T. It branches toward:

- **Expectancy**, through an arrow labeled `condition`;
- **Risk Management**, through an unlabeled arrow.

### Canonical reconstruction

Entry should be understood as more than a price coordinate. In this graph, an entry creates both:

1. a conditional expected path; and
2. a defined exposure that must be bounded.

No universal entry formula is contained in the ontology nodes themselves. Instrument-specific entry rules belong in separate playbooks.

## 2.4 Expectancy

Expectancy receives the entry’s `condition`. It then points to:

- **Alignment**, with the label `present truth`;
- **Delivery**, without an explicit label.

### Canonical reconstruction

Expectancy is the conditional path implied by a valid entry. It is not merely average profit per trade. In the graph, it appears to represent what should presently be true if the initiated sequence remains valid.

This creates an important distinction:

- **Statistical expectancy:** average outcome over many observations.
- **State expectancy:** the path or condition that should now be present.

The graph most strongly supports the second meaning.

## 2.5 Risk management

Risk Management receives a direct connection from Entry and points to Delivery through an arrow labeled `definitive zone`.

### Canonical reconstruction

Risk management defines the boundary beyond which the expected delivery is no longer treated as the same state. The phrase `definitive zone` suggests that invalidation should be attached to a structural zone, not an arbitrary monetary amount.

However, the ontology does not specify the actual zone. That value must come from the active strategy or range model.

## 2.6 Delivery

Delivery receives:

- the expected path from Expectancy;
- the definitive-zone constraint from Risk Management.

Delivery then points to Market Structure with the label `patterns`.

### Canonical reconstruction

Delivery is the realized path of an initiated sequence inside its valid risk boundary. It is observable through patterns that become legible as market structure.

This is an execution-specific meaning of “delivery.” It must remain separate from:

- foundational delivery as a broad theory term;
- session delivery, especially the New York delivery role;
- target delivery in instrument-specific playbooks.

## 2.7 Alignment

Alignment receives the `present truth` from Expectancy. It points to:

- Market Structure;
- Closure through an arrow labeled `state`.

### Canonical reconstruction

Alignment is the agreement between the expected state and presently observed market information. It is not necessarily multi-timeframe alignment, basket alignment, or indicator confluence unless a playbook defines it that way.

The graph implies that alignment has two functions:

1. it contributes to the interpretation of market structure;
2. it conditions the closure state directly.

## 2.8 Market structure

Market Structure receives both Delivery (`patterns`) and Alignment. It then points to Closure through an arrow labeled `reflexive`.

### Canonical reconstruction

Market structure is the visible organization of delivered patterns under the present alignment. Its relationship to closure is reflexive: the completion or failure of structure reveals closure, while the closure state helps determine how the structure should be interpreted.

This definition is deliberately broader than HH/HL/LH/LL structure. Those may be implementations, but they are not specified in this ontology node.

## 2.9 Closure

Closure is the terminal state of this graph branch. It receives:

- `state` from Alignment;
- `reflexive` structure from Market Structure.

It also connects outward to the missing linked file `Untitled.md`, suggesting that a fuller closure definition may exist outside the uploaded canvas.

### Canonical reconstruction

Closure is the resolved state produced when the observed structure and present alignment establish that the active sequence has completed, failed, transitioned, or been absorbed into another lattice.

The exact closure classes are not supplied. Candidate classes for later validation include completion, invalidation, re-key, containment, and transition—but these must not be promoted to canonical categories without source support.

# Chapter 3 — Basket Intelligence Architecture

**Status:** Canonical reconstruction draft

## 3.1 Purpose of the basket architecture

The canvas does not treat an FX pair as an isolated market. It organizes instruments into overlapping baskets that answer different questions:

1. **What is the dollar doing?** — USD Power Index.
2. **Is the market risk-on or risk-off?** — Safe Haven Triangle.
3. **Which European currency is expressing the move?** — European Basket.
4. **How is New York liquidity preparing to deliver?** — North America Basket.
5. **Do risk and economic-flow readings agree?** — Combined alignment layer.

The central integration node states:

> Section 1 (JPY–CHF–USD) tells you **risk**. Section 2 (EUR–GBP–USD) tells you **economy**. When they agree, the canvas expects larger weekly swings; when they disagree, it expects chop and fakeouts.

This is the basket system's governing idea: **do not choose a trade from one pair; first determine whether independent baskets tell the same story.**

---

## 3.2 USD Power Index — core dollar flow

The canvas places three instruments at the top of the dollar hierarchy:

| Instrument | Reconstructed role | Question answered |
|---|---|---|
| DXY | Broad dollar reference | Is the dollar generally appreciating or depreciating? |
| USD/JPY | Dollar plus carry/risk expression | Is dollar movement aligned with global risk and JPY flow? |
| USD/CHF | Independent dollar-strength filter | Is USD strength genuine, or is USD/JPY being driven mainly by JPY? |

The canvas summarizes USD/CHF as the **judge**:

- USD/CHF rising → canvas interpretation: USD strong.
- USD/CHF falling → canvas interpretation: USD weak.
- It is used to prevent a JPY-driven move in USD/JPY from being mistaken for a pure USD move.

### Canonical dollar-state rule

```text
INPUTS: DXY, USD/JPY, USD/CHF

IF broad dollar direction and USD/CHF agree,
THEN classify the USD reading as confirmed.

IF USD/JPY moves but USD/CHF does not confirm,
THEN treat the move as potentially JPY-led rather than USD-led.
```

This is a reconstructed classification rule, not a validated trading signal.

## 3.3 Safe Haven Triangle — the tension meter

The safe-haven layer is built from:

- CHF/JPY — “wash meter” and relative hedge contest.
- JPY Index — global/Asian hedge pressure.
- CHF Index — European hedge pressure.

The triangle is intended to answer four questions:

1. Is stress present?
2. Is the stress European or global?
3. Is CHF or JPY winning the hedge flow?
4. Is the observed USD move real or false?

### Three-pair operational core

The canvas simplifies the risk engine into three major pairs:

| Pair | Canvas role | Provisional reading |
|---|---|---|
| USD/JPY | Risk-on engine | Rising = risk-on; falling = risk-off |
| CHF/JPY | Pure risk barometer / wash meter | Rising = money leaving JPY; falling = money entering JPY |
| USD/CHF | USD strength filter | Separates true USD flow from JPY/CHF-driven movement |

### Risk rotation sequence

The canvas proposes a sequence rather than a simultaneous move:

```text
RISK-OFF:
JPY first → CHF second → USD third

RISK-ON:
USD first → CHF → JPY last
```

This sequence should remain a hypothesis until it is formally timestamp-tested across instruments.

### Canonical risk-state rule

```text
IF CHF/JPY falls materially,
AND USD/JPY also falls,
THEN classify the basket as risk-off aligned.

IF CHF/JPY rises materially,
AND USD/JPY also rises,
THEN classify the basket as risk-on aligned.

USE USD/CHF to determine whether USD independently confirms the state.
```

## 3.4 European Basket — economy and London expression

The core European basket contains:

- EUR/USD
- GBP/USD
- EUR/GBP

The expanded version adds:

- EUR/CHF
- GBP/JPY

The canvas assigns the following roles:

| Instrument | Role |
|---|---|
| EUR/USD | Weekly European-versus-dollar tone |
| GBP/USD | Higher-volatility expression of European direction |
| EUR/GBP | Internal strength selector between EUR and GBP |
| EUR/CHF | European risk/hedge meter |
| GBP/JPY | UK risk-appetite expression |

The canvas frames EUR as the anchor, GBP as a leveraged/aggressive version, and CHF as the protective version.

### EUR/GBP selection rule

```text
IF EUR/GBP rises,
THEN classify EUR as stronger than GBP
AND prefer EUR/USD as the European expression, subject to USD confirmation.

IF EUR/GBP falls,
THEN classify GBP as stronger than EUR
AND prefer GBP/USD as the European expression, subject to USD confirmation.
```

### Trend-versus-tension rule

```text
IF EUR/GBP trends,
THEN treat the London direction as more credible.

IF EUR/GBP is sideways or tightly coiled,
THEN treat European direction as unresolved
AND use CHF/JPY and CAD-linked instruments to read the release path.
```

The canvas specifically claims that European tension may appear first in CAD, then CHF, then EUR, while GBP exaggerates the eventual move. This remains a research hypothesis.

## 3.5 North Atlantic Triangle — political-economy integration

The North Atlantic system combines:

- USD — reserve, liquidity, energy-pricing and Treasury axis.
- CAD — thinner-liquidity proxy connected to U.S. credit and oil.
- EUR — European settlement axis.
- GBP — aggressive/high-beta European expression.
- CHF — European settlement hedge.

This system bridges the European and North American baskets. Its core proposition is that **CAD may reveal developing North American positioning before the larger USD pairs fully move.**

### Reconstructed sequencing model

```text
EUR/GBP tension or neutrality
        ↓
CAD reacts first or acts as proxy
        ↓
CHF confirms the hedge direction
        ↓
USD delivers through NY pairs
        ↓
GBP exaggerates; EUR follows more slowly
```

The canvas summarizes this as:

> Watch CAD → position GBP → hedge with CHF.

Because several causal claims in this section are broad, they must remain `research_reference_only` until tested.

## 3.8 Daily basket drill

The strongest operational sequence recovered from the canvas is:

1. **Read EUR/GBP** — determine European internal strength or unresolved tension.
2. **Read USD/CAD/CAD flow** — identify possible early NY positioning.
3. **Read USD/CHF** — confirm genuine USD direction.
4. **Read CHF/JPY and USD/JPY** — confirm risk-on/risk-off state.
5. **Select the expression** — EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/CAD, USD/JPY, or CHF/JPY.
6. **Wait for session confirmation** — Asia reveals, London confirms or conflicts, NY delivers or invalidates.

### Formalized decision skeleton

```text
STATE_EUROPE = trend direction from EUR/GBP, or unresolved
STATE_CAD = early NY proxy direction, or neutral
STATE_USD = confirmed/contradicted by DXY and USD/CHF
STATE_RISK = risk-on/risk-off/mixed from CHF/JPY and USD/JPY

IF STATE_EUROPE, STATE_USD, and STATE_RISK align,
THEN select the pair expressing the strongest relative divergence.

IF one or more states conflict,
THEN downgrade confidence or wait for NY resolution.
```

# Chapter 4 — Session Architecture: Truth, Confirmation, and Delivery

**Source basis:** Canvas nodes 23, 27–28, 34–37, 41–42

## 4.1 The three-session state machine

The canvas organizes the trading day as a handoff:

```text
ASIA — Truth / first bias
        ↓
LONDON — Confirmation, conflict, or hedge selection
        ↓
NEW YORK — Delivery, continuation, squeeze, or invalidation
```

This is not merely chronological. Each session has a different epistemic role:

- **Asia reveals** the first risk signal through JPY-linked instruments.
- **London tests** that signal through CHF and European cross-flows.
- **New York resolves** whether USD and North American liquidity confirm or reverse it.

---

## 4.2 Asian Session — truth session

Primary instruments:

- USD/JPY
- CHF/JPY
- Supporting context: AUD/JPY, NZD/JPY, WTI/CAD where relevant

The canvas expects Asia to provide:

- The first meaningful bias of the day.
- The cleanest early read on risk sentiment.
- A direction London may respect, challenge, or overshoot.

### Asian classification rule

```text
IF CHF/JPY falls
AND USD/JPY falls,
THEN classify Asia as risk-off aligned.

IF CHF/JPY rises
AND USD/JPY rises,
THEN classify Asia as risk-on aligned.

IF the two disagree,
THEN classify Asia as mixed and await London.
```

No magnitude threshold is provided in the canvas; therefore the rule is incomplete for automation.

---

## 4.3 London Session — confirmation and hedge selection

Primary instruments:

- CHF/JPY
- EUR/GBP
- EUR/USD
- GBP/USD
- EUR/CHF

The canvas assigns London two jobs:

1. Determine whether European money accepts the Asian risk direction.
2. Determine whether CHF or JPY is the preferred hedge and whether EUR or GBP is the preferred European expression.

### London response states

```text
IF CHF follows the JPY direction established in Asia,
THEN classify London as confirming
AND elevate the probability of a trend day.

IF CHF opposes JPY,
THEN classify London as conflicting
AND downgrade the day toward chop.

IF CHF materially overshoots the Asian signal,
THEN classify the move as a possible reversal or weekly-extreme event.
```

“Overshoot” is undefined in the source and must be quantified before execution.

### European internal selector

```text
IF EUR/GBP rises during the accepted London direction,
THEN EUR is the preferred European leg.

IF EUR/GBP falls,
THEN GBP is the preferred European leg.

IF EUR/GBP remains compressed,
THEN treat Europe as unresolved and rely more heavily on CHF/JPY and CAD/USD confirmation.
```

## 4.4 New York Session — delivery session

Primary instruments:

- USD/JPY
- USD/CHF
- EUR/USD
- GBP/USD
